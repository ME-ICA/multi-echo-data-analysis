Make design matrices¶

For TEDPCA and TEDICA, we use regression to get parameter estimates (PEs; not beta values) for component time-series against echo-specific data, and substitute those PEs for Sˉ(TEk){\bar{S}(TE_k)}Sˉ(TEk​). At some point, I would like to dig into why those parameter estimates are equivalent to Sˉ(TEk){\bar{S}(TE_k)}Sˉ(TEk​) for our purposes.

TE-independence model¶

ΔS(TEk)S(TEk)ˉ=ΔS0S0\frac{{\Delta}S(TE_k)}{\bar{S(TE_k)}} = \frac{{\Delta}S_0}{S_0}S(TEk​)ˉ​ΔS(TEk​)​=S0​ΔS0​​
(2)
ΔS(TEk)=Sˉ(TEk)ΔS0S0{\Delta}S(TE_k) = {\bar{S}(TE_k)}\frac{{\Delta}S_0}{S_0}ΔS(TEk​)=Sˉ(TEk​)S0​ΔS0​​
(3)

ΔS0S0\frac{{\Delta}S_0}{S_0}S0​ΔS0​​ is a scalar (i.e., doesn’t change with TE), so we ignore that, which means we only use Sˉ(TEk){\bar{S}(TE_k)}Sˉ(TEk​) (mean echo-wise signal).

Thus,

ΔS(TEk)=Sˉ(TEk)∗X{\Delta}S(TE_k) = {\bar{S}(TE_k)} * XΔS(TEk​)=Sˉ(TEk​)∗X
(4)

and for TEDPCA/TEDICA,

PE(TEk)=Sˉ(TEk)∗XPE(TE_k) = {\bar{S}(TE_k)} * XPE(TEk​)=Sˉ(TEk​)∗X
(5)

Lastly, we fit X to the data and evaluate model fit.

TE-dependence model¶

ΔS(TEk)Sˉ(TEk)=−ΔR2∗∗TEk\frac{{\Delta}S(TE_k)}{\bar{S}(TE_k)} = -{\Delta}{R_2^*}*TE_kSˉ(TEk​)ΔS(TEk​)​=−ΔR2∗​∗TEk​
(6)
ΔS(TEk)=Sˉ(TEk)∗−ΔR2∗∗TEk{\Delta}S(TE_k) = {\bar{S}(TE_k)} * -{\Delta}{R_2^*}*TE_kΔS(TEk​)=Sˉ(TEk​)∗−ΔR2∗​∗TEk​
(7)

−ΔR2∗-{\Delta}{R_2^*}−ΔR2∗​ is a scalar, so we ignore it, which means we only use Sˉ(TEk){\bar{S}(TE_k)}Sˉ(TEk​) (mean echo-wise-signal) and TEkTE_kTEk​ (echo time in milliseconds).

Thus,

ΔS(TEk)=Sˉ(TEk)∗TEk∗X{\Delta}S(TE_k) = {\bar{S}(TE_k)}*TE_k * XΔS(TEk​)=Sˉ(TEk​)∗TEk​∗X
(8)

and for TEDPCA/TEDICA,

PE(TEk)=Sˉ(TEk)∗TEk∗XPE(TE_k) = {\bar{S}(TE_k)}*TE_k * XPE(TEk​)=Sˉ(TEk​)∗TEk​∗X
(9)

Lastly, we fit X to the data and evaluate model fit.

Algorithm 1 (Minimum image regression)

Inputs

  • O\mathbf{O}O is the matrix of optimally combined (OC) data, of shape v×tv \times tv×t, where vvv is the number of voxels in the brain mask and ttt is the number of timepoints in the scan.

  • M\mathbf{M}M is the mixing matrix from the ICA decomposition, of shape c×tc \times tc×t, where ccc is the number of components.

  • WWW is the set of indices of all components in M\mathbf{M}M: W={1,2,3,...,c}W = \{1, 2, 3, ..., c\}W={1,2,3,...,c}

  • NNN is the set of indices of all non-ignored components (i.e., all accepted or BOLD-like, and rejected or non-BOLD components) in M\mathbf{M}M: N∈Nk s.t 1≤k≤c,N⊆WN \in \mathbb{N}^k \text{ s.t } 1 \leq k \leq c, N \subseteq WN∈Nk s.t 1≤k≤c,N⊆W

  • AAA is the set of indices of all accepted (i.e., BOLD-like) components in M\mathbf{M}M: A∈Nl s.t 1≤l≤k,A⊆NA \in \mathbb{N}^l \text{ s.t } 1 \leq l \leq k, A \subseteq NA∈Nl s.t 1≤l≤k,A⊆N

Outputs

  • Multi-echo denoised data without the T1-like effect, referred to as D\mathbf{D}D or MEDN+MIR.

  • Multi-echo BOLD-like data without the T1-like effect, referred to as H\mathbf{H}H or MEHK+MIR.

  • ICA mixing matrix with the T1-like effect removed from component time series (K\mathbf{K}K).

  • Map of the T1-like effect (m\mathbf{m}m)

Algorithm

  1. The voxel-wise means (O‾∈Rv\mathbf{\overline{O}} \in \mathbb{R}^{v}O∈Rv) and standard deviations (σO∈Rv\mathbf{\sigma_{O}} \in \mathbb{R}^{v}σO​∈Rv) of the optimally combined data are computed over time.

  2. The optimally combined data are z-normalized over time (Oz∈Rv×t\mathbf{O_z} \in \mathbb{R}^{v \times t}Oz​∈Rv×t).

  3. The normalized optimally combined data matrix (Oz\mathbf{O_z}Oz​) is regressed on the ICA mixing matrix (M∈Rc×t\mathbf{M} \in \mathbb{R}^{c \times t}M∈Rc×t) to construct component-wise parameter estimate maps (B∈Rv×c\mathbf{B} \in \mathbb{R}^{v \times c}B∈Rv×c).

    Oz=BM+ϵ,ϵ∈Rv×t\mathbf{O_{z}} = \mathbf{B} \mathbf{M} + \mathbf{\epsilon}, \enspace \mathbf{\epsilon} \in \mathbb{R}^{v \times t}Oz​=BM+ϵ,ϵ∈Rv×t
    (10)

  4. NNN is used to select rows from the mixing matrix M\mathbf{M}M and columns from the parameter estimate matrix B\mathbf{B}B that correspond to non-ignored (i.e., accepted and rejected) components, forming reduced matrices MN\mathbf{M}_NMN​ and BN\mathbf{B}_NBN​. The normalized time series matrix for the combined ignored components and variance left unexplained by the ICA decomposition is then computed by subtracting the scalar product of the non-ignored beta weight and mixing matrices from the normalized OC data time series (Oz\mathbf{O_{z}}Oz​). The result is referred to as the normalized residuals time series matrix (R∈Rv×t\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{v \times t}R∈Rv×t).

    R=Oz−BNMN,BN∈Rv×∣N∣,MN∈R∣N∣×t\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{O_{z}} - \mathbf{B}_N \mathbf{M}_N, \enspace \mathbf{B}_N \in \mathbb{R}^{v \times |N|}, \enspace \mathbf{M}_N \in \mathbb{R}^{|N| \times t}R=Oz​−BN​MN​,BN​∈Rv×∣N∣,MN​∈R∣N∣×t
    (11)

  5. We can likewise construct the normalized time series of BOLD-like components (P∈Rv×t\mathbf{P} \in \mathbb{R}^{v \times t}P∈Rv×t) by multiplying similarly reduced parameter estimate and mixing matrices composed of only the columns and rows, respectively, that are associated with the accepted components indexed in AAA. The resulting time series matrix is similar to the time series matrix referred to elsewhere in the manuscript as multi-echo high-Kappa (MEHK), with the exception that the component time series have been normalized prior to reconstruction.

    P=BAMA,BA∈Rv×∣A∣,MA∈R∣A∣×t\mathbf{P} = \mathbf{B}_A \mathbf{M}_A, \enspace \mathbf{B}_A \in \mathbb{R}^{v \times |A|}, \enspace \mathbf{M}_A \in \mathbb{R}^{|A| \times t}P=BA​MA​,BA​∈Rv×∣A∣,MA​∈R∣A∣×t
    (12)

  6. The map of the T1-like effect (m∈Rv\mathbf{m} \in \mathbb{R}^{v}m∈Rv) is constructed by taking the minimum across timepoints from the normalized MEHK time series (P\mathbf{P}P) and then mean-centering across brain voxels. Let J={1,...,t}J = \{1, ..., t\}J={1,...,t} denote the indices of the columns of matrix P\mathbf{P}P, and let pijp_{ij}pij​ denote the value of the element P[i,j]\mathbf{P}[i,j]P[i,j].

    qi=min⁡j∈Jpij∀i=1,...,v\mathbf{q_{i}} = \min_{j\in{J}}p_{ij} \quad \forall i = 1,...,vqi​=j∈Jmin​pij​∀i=1,...,v
    (13)
    m=q−q‾,q∈Rv\mathbf{m} = \mathbf{q} - \mathbf{\overline{q}}, \enspace \mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{R}^{v}m=q−q​,q∈Rv
    (14)

  7. The standardized optimally combined time series matrix (Oz\mathbf{O_z}Oz​) is regressed on the T1-like effect map (m\mathbf{m}m) to estimate the volume-wise global signal time series (g∈Rt\mathbf{g} \in \mathbb{R}^tg∈Rt).

    Oz=m⊗g+ϵ,ϵ∈Rv×t\mathbf{O_{z}} = \mathbf{m} \otimes \mathbf{g} + \mathbf{\epsilon}, \enspace \mathbf{\epsilon} \in \mathbb{R}^{v \times t}Oz​=m⊗g+ϵ,ϵ∈Rv×t
    (15)

    Where ⊗\otimes⊗ is the outer product.

  8. The normalized BOLD time series matrix (P\mathbf{P}P) is then regressed on this global signal time series (g\mathbf{g}g) in order to estimate a global signal map (s∈Rv\mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{R}^vs∈Rv) and the normalized BOLD time series matrix without the T1-like effect (E∈Rv×t\mathbf{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{v \times t}E∈Rv×t).

    P=g⊗s+E\mathbf{P} = \mathbf{g} \otimes \mathbf{s} + \mathbf{E}P=g⊗s+E
    (16)

  9. The time series matrix of BOLD-like components without the T1-like effect (MEHK+MIR, H∈Rv×t\mathbf{H} \in \mathbb{R}^{v \times t}H∈Rv×t), scaled to match the original OC time series matrix, is constructed by multiplying each column of E\mathbf{E}E by the vector σO\mathbf{\sigma_{O}}σO​.

    H=E∘(σO1⋯σO1⋮⋮⋮σOv⋯σOv)⏟t\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{E} \circ \underbrace{ \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{{\sigma_{O}}_1} & \cdots & \mathbf{{\sigma_{O}}_1}\\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\ \mathbf{{\sigma_{O}}_v} & \cdots & \mathbf{{\sigma_{O}}_v}\\ \end{pmatrix} }_{t}H=E∘t⎝⎛​σO​1​⋮σO​v​​⋯⋮⋯​σO​1​⋮σO​v​​⎠⎞​​​
    (17)

    Where ∘ is the Hadamard product for element-wise multiplication of two matrices.

  10. The ICA-denoised time series without the T1-like effect (MEDN+MIR, D∈Rv×t\mathbf{D} \in \mathbb{R}^{v \times t}D∈Rv×t) is constructed by adding the residuals time series (R\mathbf{R}R) to the normalized BOLD time series (E\mathbf{E}E), multiplying each column of the result by the vector σO\sigma_{O}σO​, and adding back in the voxel-wise mean of the OC time series (O‾\mathbf{\overline{O}}O).

    D=O‾+(E+R)∘(σO1⋯σO1⋮⋮⋮σOv⋯σOv)⏟t\mathbf{D} = \mathbf{\overline{O}} + (\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{R}) \circ \underbrace{ \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{{\sigma_{O}}_1} & \cdots & \mathbf{{\sigma_{O}}_1}\\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\ \mathbf{{\sigma_{O}}_v} & \cdots & \mathbf{{\sigma_{O}}_v}\\ \end{pmatrix} }_{t}D=O+(E+R)∘t⎝⎛​σO​1​⋮σO​v​​⋯⋮⋯​σO​1​⋮σO​v​​⎠⎞​​​
    (18)

  11. The T1c-corrected ICA mixing matrix is then derived by regressing the global signal time series g\mathbf{g}g from each component’s time series. Let Q\mathbf{Q}Q be the associated parameter estimate matrix (Q∈Rc×t\mathbf{Q} \in \mathbb{R}^{c \times t}Q∈Rc×t).

    M=Qg+K\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{g} + \mathbf{K}M=Qg+K
    (19)
    K=M−Qg\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{M} - \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{g}K=M−Qg
    (20)
